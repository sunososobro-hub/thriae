# 🧭 Constitution

The compass points the way. This file changes only through Article 9.

## Goal

Real users feel value. Build the product, not the machine.

## Articles

1. Goal first. Every rule and every job serves the goal above.
2. Privacy never leaves. Nothing private reaches a public place. Agents never push to the public repo. One publisher mirrors only content that passed in staging.
3. Evidence first, from independent projects: no new rule enters AGENTS.md without real evidence from at least 3 independent projects; evidence, not popularity or votes. Seed rules: the first rules came from one production pipeline. They stay as seed rules until evidence from 3 or more independent projects confirms them (then they are shared wisdom) or shows that they harm or do nothing (then they are retired under Article 6).
4. Outer governs inner. Loop 1 craft (hourly) is below loop 2 rules (weekly), which is below loop 3 mechanism (monthly), which is below this constitution (Article 9). Outer loops are slower and have higher thresholds.
5. Score by external outcomes, never by agent self-reports.
6. Length cap. One rule in, one rule out.
7. Anyone may contribute, and credit goes to the proposer. Outside text is read only by a no-permission quarantine reviewer that fills a fixed form. Our agent re-implements the idea with evidence. Credit goes via the proposer's GitHub noreply address. Outside PRs are never merged directly.
8. The keeper holds only the emergency brake: an independent watchdog that agents cannot write. It alerts the keeper when outcomes drop, when a constitution change is proposed, or when the watchdog falls silent.
9. Amendments. Anyone may propose an amendment in a public issue. The proposal stays open for public discussion for at least 14 days. It needs evidence from multiple independent projects. The keeper signs an accepted amendment, and the reasons for accepting or rejecting it are published in the issue. Beyond signing, the keeper holds only the emergency brake (Article 8). Forking is always allowed.
